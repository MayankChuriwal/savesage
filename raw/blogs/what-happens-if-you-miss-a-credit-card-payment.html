What Happens If You Miss a Credit Card Payment? (2026 Guide)

Missing a credit card payment can be stressful but don't panic. If you missed your credit card payment even by just 1 day, the best thing to do is to pay the outstanding amount as soon as possible through your bank's mobile app or net banking.

20 Jul 2026

Under Reserve Bank of India (RBI) guidelines, banks generally cannot levy late payment charges or report your missed payment to credit information companies until it remains overdue for more than three days after the due date. However, interest may still apply according to your card issuer's terms and conditions. 

Here's what happens if your payment is delayed by 1 day, more than 3 days, around 30 days or even 90 days, along with the steps you should take to minimise penalties and protect your credit score.

Missed Your Credit Card Payment by 1 Day?

If you forgot to pay your credit card bill by one day, take these steps immediately :

  • Pay the outstanding amount as soon as possible.

  • If you can't pay the full bill, pay at least the minimum amount due so that it won’t affect your credit score.

  • Keep a copy of payment confirmation.

  • If this is your first missed payment, contact your bank after making the payment. Some issuers may reverse the late payment fee as a one-time goodwill gesture. 

A one-day delay generally doesn't affect your credit score if you clear the dues quickly. However, depending on your card issuer's policy, finance charges may still apply because many banks calculate interest from the transaction date if the full statement balance isn't paid by the due date. 

What Happens as the Delay Increases?

Up to 3 Days After the Due Date

This is commonly referred to as the RBI 3-day rule. Under RBI guidelines, banks generally cannot levy late payment charges or report your account until the payment remains overdue for more than three days after the due date. 

RBI's Three-Day Rule Does Not Mean

  • You automatically get a three-day interest-free extension.

  • Your payment due date changes.

  • All penalties are waived.

However, this doesn't mean your payment is free of interest. If you don't pay the total amount due by the due date, many card issuers calculate finance charges from the date of each transaction, not from the due date, as per their Most Important Terms and Conditions (MITC). Even a short delay can increase the interest you pay. 

More Than 3 Days After the Due Date

If you still haven't paid after three days, here's what may happen: 

  • Late payment charges may be levied.

  • GST applies on late payment fees and interest.

  • Finance charges continue until the outstanding amount is repaid.

  • You may lose the interest-free period on new purchases until the outstanding balance is cleared.

Around 30 Days After the Due Date

If your bill remains unpaid for around 30 days, your card issuer may report the overdue account to credit information companies such as CIBIL as part of its reporting cycle. A reported missed payment can lower your credit score and remain on your credit report for up to 36 months.

More Than 90 Days After the Due Date

A prolonged default can have serious consequences. Your bank may block your card, begin recovery proceedings and continue charging applicable interest and penalties. Your credit score can also drop significantly, making it much harder to get approved for future loans or credit cards.

A lower credit score can make it harder to:

  • Get approved for a new credit card.

  • Qualify for personal, home or car loans.

  • Receive higher credit limits.

  • Access better interest rates from lenders.

If you're struggling to repay the outstanding amount, contact your bank as early as possible to discuss available repayment options rather than ignoring the dues. 

Can You Still Use Your Credit Card After Missing a Payment?

It depends on how long the payment remains overdue and your card issuer's policies.

A short delay doesn't usually result in your credit card being blocked immediately. However, if the outstanding balance isn't cleared, your bank may:

  • Reduce your available credit limit.

  • Decline new transactions.

  • Temporarily suspend your card.

  • Permanently block the card in case of prolonged default.

Paying the overdue amount as early as possible reduces the chances of these restrictions.

Most Indian credit card issuers typically charge finance charges of around 3% to 4% per month (roughly 36% to 48% per annum), although the exact rate depends on your card issuer and card variant. Always check your card's Most Important Terms and Conditions (MITC) for the applicable rate. 

Minimum Amount Due vs Total Amount Due

Paying only the minimum amount due helps you avoid missing the payment completely but it doesn't stop interest from being charged on the remaining balance. 

For example, if your outstanding balance is ₹50,000 and the minimum amount due is ₹2,500, paying only ₹2,500 means interest can continue on the remaining ₹47,500. New purchases may also lose the interest-free period.

Whenever possible, pay the total outstanding amount instead of just the minimum due.

How to Avoid Missing Future Credit Card Payments

  • Enable AutoPay for the total amount due or minimum amount due.

  • Set payment reminders a few days before the due date.

  • Turn on SMS and email payment alerts from your bank.

  • Review your monthly statements regularly.

  • Keep enough funds in your linked bank account before the AutoPay date to avoid payment failures.

FAQs

1. Can I get my late payment fee waived?

If this is your first missed payment and you've otherwise maintained a good repayment history, your bank may consider reversing the late payment fee as a one-time goodwill gesture. Approval is entirely at the issuer's discretion.

2. What happens if I miss my credit card payment by one day?

A one-day delay generally won't affect your credit score if you clear the payment quickly. However, interest may still apply depending on your card issuer's terms and conditions. Pay the outstanding amount as soon as possible to minimise charges.

3. Does paying the minimum amount due stop interest?

No. Paying the minimum amount due helps prevent your account from becoming completely unpaid, but interest usually continues on the remaining outstanding balance. You may also lose the interest-free period on new purchases until the dues are cleared. 

4. What should I do immediately after missing my credit card due date?

Pay the outstanding amount as soon as possible. If you can't pay the full balance, pay at least the minimum amount due, save the payment confirmation and monitor your next statement for any applicable interest or late payment charges. If it's your first missed payment, you can also request your bank to consider waiving the late fee. 

5. Can my credit card be blocked if I miss a payment?

A short delay usually won't result in your credit card being blocked. However, if your payment remains overdue for an extended period, your bank may reduce your credit limit, suspend new transactions or block the card until the outstanding amount is paid.
