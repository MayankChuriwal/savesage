Axis Bank Credit Card Eligibility

Applying for an Axis Bank credit card without checking eligibility first is one of the most common mistakes people make. Every credit card application can leave a hard inquiry on your credit report, and too many inquiries in a short period can make the next approval harder. Your age, income, credit score, employment type, residential status, existing EMIs and documents all play a part, so check whether your details are consistent with what the specific card expects before you apply.

Income Requirements by Card Type

Axis Bank income requirements vary significantly depending on which card you are applying for. The basic income requirement can start from ₹15,000 per month for entry-level cards, while premium cards usually need higher income, a stronger credit profile and clean repayment history.

One should always check the card’s specific eligibility before applying. A card like SELECT, Atlas or Magnus can have very different requirements from an entry-level card, even though all are issued by Axis Bank.

For salaried applicants, Axis may ask for salary slips, salary bank statements, Form 16 or similar income proof. For self-employed applicants, the bank may ask for Income Tax Returns, computation of income, business bank statements, certified financial documents or proof that the business is active.

Documents Required for Axis Bank Credit Card

Documents can vary depending on the card and applicant profile, but these are commonly required:

  • PAN card or Form 60

  • Age and residential proof such as Aadhaar card, passport, voter ID or driving licence

  • Income proof such as salary slips, bank statements, Form 16 or Income Tax Returns

  • Computation of income, business or personal bank statements, if required for self-employed applicants

  • Proof of business continuity, if required

  • Passport-sized photograph, if required during application

  • Mobile number linked to Aadhaar, if required for digital verification

  • Fixed deposit details, if you are applying for an Axis secured credit card backed by an FD 

Form 60 is used when an applicant does not have a PAN. Form 16 is an annual tax certificate issued by an employer, usually used by salaried applicants as income proof.

If you already have an Axis Bank account, your KYC may already be on record with the bank, which can make the document process smoother.

Credit Score and Approval Chances

Axis’s general eligibility guidance mentions above 750 as a preferred credit score, but it does not publish separate score cut-offs for every card. If this is your first card, our How to Get Your First Credit Card in India guide can help you understand the basics before you choose between entry-level, cashback, secured or travel-focused Axis cards.

A lower score may still be considered for entry-level cards if your income, repayment history and documents are strong. Premium cards usually need a stronger overall income picture, which means good income, clean repayment history and manageable EMIs along with a healthy score.

If you have no credit history, getting an unsecured Axis credit card can be difficult because the bank has no repayment record to judge. In that case, an FD-backed card, add-on card or existing-bank offer may be an easier starting point.

Existing Axis Bank Relationship

Having a salary account, savings account, fixed deposit or investment relationship with Axis Bank can help because the bank may already have a view of your income credits, balance pattern and account behaviour.

You may also see pre-approved offers for specific cards based on your account history in the Axis Bank app, net banking or bank messages. This usually means Axis has already checked some part of your profile, so the application may be smoother. It is still not final approval; KYC, documents and final checks may still be required.

Which Axis Card Should Beginners Start With?

If this is your first credit card, avoid starting with a premium Axis card only because the rewards look attractive. If this is your first card, understand how first credit cards work in India before choosing between entry-level, cashback, secured or travel-focused Axis cards.

A better starting point could be:

  • an entry-level Axis credit card such as Axis MY Zone or Axis ACE, suited to first-time or moderate-income users

  • a cashback card such as Axis Flipkart or Axis ACE, for regular online spends, bill payments or everyday purchases

  • a card offered through your existing Axis relationship, often shown as a pre-approved offer in Axis net banking or the mobile app

  • a secured or FD-backed Axis credit card if you have no credit history

  • a premium travel card such as Axis Atlas, only if you already have strong income, good credit history and travel-focused spending

A secured Axis credit card is usually backed by a fixed deposit, making FD-backed credit cards a practical option for users with no credit history or limited income proof.

Common Reasons Axis Credit Card Applications Get Rejected

Most rejections fall into one of three buckets:

Profile mismatch

  • Age or residential status not matching the card’s eligibility rules

  • Low or unstable income

  • Applying for a card above your current income level

Credit history issues

  • No credit history

  • Low credit score

  • Recent missed payments or defaults

  • Too many credit applications in a short time

  • High existing EMIs, loans or credit card dues

Application errors

  • PAN, Aadhaar, address or mobile number mismatch

  • Incomplete or unclear income documents

  • Internal bank policy or risk checks

Sometimes rejection does not mean you can never get an Axis credit card. It may simply mean the selected card was not suitable for your current profile.

How to Improve Your Approval Chances

Before applying, check your credit score, active EMIs, credit card dues and documents. You can check your credit score for free on the SaveSage app before applying, so you know where your profile stands before risking another hard inquiry.

If your score is below the preferred range, focus on paying bills on time and reducing outstanding dues before applying again. Choose a card that fits your current income instead of directly applying for a premium card.

Also make sure your PAN, Aadhaar, mobile number and address details are consistent across all documents, because small mismatches can delay or hurt the application. Avoid applying for multiple cards together. If your application is rejected, wait, understand the possible reason, improve your profile and then apply for a more suitable card.

FAQs

What is the age requirement for an Axis Bank credit card?

Axis requires primary cardholders to be 18–70 years old. For Burgundy customers, the maximum age limit is 80 years. Add-on cardholders are usually required to be over 18 years.

What income is needed for an Axis Bank credit card?

The minimum income requirement starts from ₹15,000 per month for entry-level cards, but individual cards may need more. For example, Axis SELECT lists ₹6 lakh per annum, Axis Atlas lists ₹12 lakh for salaried users and ₹15 lakh for self-employed users, and Axis Magnus lists ₹24 lakh annual net salary or ITR.

What documents are required for Axis Bank credit cards?

You may need PAN or Form 60, age and residential proof, income proof such as salary slips or ITR, bank statements, and extra documents depending on whether you are salaried or self-employed.

Does having an Axis salary account improve approval chances?

It can help, because Axis may already be able to see your income credits and account behaviour. However, documents and final checks may still apply.

What should I do if my Axis credit card application is rejected?

Do not apply again immediately. Check your credit score, fix document mismatches, reduce recent applications, choose a more suitable card and wait before reapplying.

Axis Bank credit card approval is not only about income or rewards. The safer approach is to check the card’s eligibility, prepare the right documents, review your credit profile and apply for a card that fits your current profile.
